The buyer journey

Every section eyebrow carries a four-node path that fills as the visitor moves through these stages:

  • Recognise: is this our problem, and why does it matter commercially?
  • Understand: why existing approaches miss it, what NeuroAIQ sees, and what LEAD and DRIVE are.
  • Believe: evidence and outcomes.
  • Act: the right next step for each level of readiness.

Reference set: the standard, not the look

  • McKinsey Insights: headlines state the finding. Applied to every Home headline.
  • Gartner: proprietary IP named consistently until it becomes vocabulary. Applied to LEAD, DRIVE and the DRIVE Scorecard.
  • Behavioural Insights Team: behavioural science made practical and commercial. Applied to how the neuroscience is placed.
  • Wellcome: authority from type scale and whitespace, not decoration. Applied to rhythm and restraint.
  • IDEO: case studies as narrative. Applied to the evidence section.
  • Stripe Press: ideas presented as crafted objects. Applied to how LEAD is shown.
  • Linear: motion that explains rather than decorates. Applied to the motion principles.
  • What none of them give us is the Pathway. That device belongs to LEAD.

The NeuroAIQ visual system: the Pathway

  • A line and nodes. Hollow nodes are conditions; the gold node is the outcome or the recommended step.
  • Used in the hero, every eyebrow, LEAD, the DRIVE diagram, the evidence narrative, the next-step ladder, and inner-page eyebrows.
  • Type: Plus Jakarta Sans, with major statements up to 104px so they carry the page.
  • Imagery: human and editorial, desaturated into navy. No robots, holograms, glowing brains or circuitry.
  • Inner pages open with a faded, blue-toned photograph on the right, so every page starts with people without competing with the headline.
  • All stock photography is from Unsplash’s free licence: real photographs, no watermarks, free for commercial use.

Motion principles

  • Three movements only: draw (a line extends), light (a node fills), settle (text rises 18px).
  • Nothing moves unless it shows a connection. LEAD draws in causal order, and the evidence reveals as a sequence.
  • Respects reduced-motion settings. It can be switched off in Tweaks.

Conversion journey

  • Recognise: “See where adoption breaks down”.
  • Understand: “Understand the Diagnostic”.
  • Believe: “Read the case study”.
  • Act: “Take the DRIVE Scorecard”, then “Book a consultation”.
  • The booking ask appears only in the header, the ladder and the closing band. It is never repeated section by section.

Challenge

Why adoption stalled

A management team was struggling with adoption. We partnered with them to find the cause.

Key findings

62 hidden barriers

We identified 62 operational pain points their metrics never showed, the organisational environment holding adoption back.

What the assessment revealed

Mapped to the brain

Using the LEAD framework, we mapped these to the workforce’s adaptive brain responses, and identified the three patterns driving low adoption.

Business implications

Structural, not resistance

68% of the low adoption came from structural organisational conditions, not individual resistance to change.

Outcome

Behaviour, not awareness

By Day 16, 78% of participants were still using our tools — a shift in behaviour, not just awareness, moving toward stronger adoption.
