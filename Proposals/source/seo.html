Why search matters for NeuroAIQ

The site's primary job is lead generation. For a firm selling to leaders who research before they reach out, being findable is where lead generation starts.

How buyers shortlist

Leaders search before they call

Before an owner or executive books a conversation, they search — for the problem, for the approach, and for the firm. If NeuroAIQ isn't in those results, it isn't on the shortlist.
The buyer guides

Missing from the guides buyers read

The team's competitor research found NeuroAIQ in none of the 2026 Australian AI consulting buyer guides reviewed. Those guides rank well and get read at shortlist stage — which is exactly when a buyer decides who to call.
AI assistants

More research now starts with an AI

A growing share of questions go to ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot instead of a search box. Those tools answer from what they can find and trust — so being findable now also means being quotable.

Part one · at launch

Foundations: fix what search engines see

The unglamorous work that everything else depends on. Nearly all of it happens once, as part of the rebuild.

  • One SEO tool, not two. On WordPress, keep one plugin and remove the other. On static hosting, the rebuild handles it directly.
  • A written title and description for every page, in NeuroAIQ's own words — never left to be generated.
  • One clean sitemap, submitted to both Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools.
  • A redirect for every old address, so links and rankings the current site has built aren't lost at launch.
  • One main heading per page, with the page's real subject in it.
  • Fast on a phone. Pass Google's Core Web Vitals: loading under 2.5 seconds, responding in under 200 milliseconds, nothing jumping around as it loads.
  • Images compressed and described — smaller files for speed, and a short description for screen readers and search.
  • A proper share image at 1200 × 627 pixels, so LinkedIn posts of the site look deliberate.
  • One address for everything — with or without www, always landing in the same place.

Proposed page titles. Each leads with what a buyer would search for and stays under 60 characters, so it isn't cut off in results. These follow the pages in the current prototype and will be adjusted to match the final sitemap.

Where the value is

Stage 1 is the widest open

These are specific questions rather than broad industry terms, so there's far less competition for them — and they come from exactly the buyer NeuroAIQ wants: someone who has already spent on AI and can't see the return. That's where most of the content in Part 3 is aimed.
A careful one

"AI change management"

Buyers search this, but NeuroAIQ isn't a change-management firm and shouldn't read like one. We'd answer it with a page explaining why adoption is a question of evidence rather than change — capturing the search without taking on the positioning.
The free alternative

Answer the SMEC AI question

Owners researching AI help will find SMEC AI, the free government-funded program. A plain comparison page — what the free option covers, and what a diagnostic adds — meets that question head-on rather than leaving it unanswered.

Part four

Showing up in AI answers, not just Google

When a leader asks ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot who can help with AI adoption, the tool doesn't show ten links. It gives an answer — and it names a few sources. The aim is for NeuroAIQ to be one of them.

Simplified. Each assistant draws on a different mix, and the mix changes — which is why the plan covers all three sources rather than betting on one.

Bing, quietly

Bing matters more than it used to

Microsoft Copilot answers from Bing; DuckDuckGo and Yahoo draw heavily on Bing's results; and ChatGPT's search has drawn on it too. Bing Webmaster Tools takes ten minutes to set up and it can import the site's setup straight from Google Search Console.
Answer first

Put the answer at the top

AI tools tend to quote pages that answer a question directly in the first few lines, then explain. Every problem page opens with a one-paragraph answer a tool could lift as it is.
Consistency

Say the same thing everywhere

AI tools cross-check. If the website, LinkedIn, the Australian Government AI Directory and Google all describe NeuroAIQ the same way, the tools are more likely to name it. Mixed descriptions make them hedge.
Named expertise

Make the founder findable

A neuroscience-trained founder recognised through Australia's Global Talent program is a genuine credential — stated plainly on the site and linked to the founder's LinkedIn, it gives an AI tool a reason to trust the advice.

Measuring AI visibility. There's no Search Console for AI answers yet, so we measure it directly. Once a month, the same set of buyer questions goes to ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot and Perplexity, and we record whether NeuroAIQ is named. Visits arriving from those tools are tracked separately in Google Analytics.

From the monthly question set
  • Why isn't our AI investment delivering value?
  • How do I measure AI adoption across a business?
  • Who helps Australian organisations with AI adoption?
  • What is an AI adoption diagnostic?
How AI assistants find sources changes often — this section is worth rechecking every six months.

Letting the right crawlers in

AI companies send separate crawlers for separate jobs. Some fetch pages to answer a question right now; others collect pages to train future models. They can be allowed or blocked individually, in the site's robots.txt file.

CrawlerCompanyWhat it's forRecommendation
GooglebotGoogleSearch results and AI OverviewsAllow
BingbotMicrosoftBing results and CopilotAllow
OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-UserOpenAIChatGPT's search, and pages a user asks it to openAllow
Claude-SearchBot, Claude-UserAnthropicClaude's search, and pages a user asks it to openAllow
PerplexityBotPerplexityPerplexity's answersAllow
GPTBot, ClaudeBot, Google-ExtendedVariousTraining future AI models — not searchYour call

Today everything is allowed, which is the right starting point. Training crawlers are a genuine choice: allowing them helps future models know about NeuroAIQ; blocking them keeps the content out of training. Blocking them doesn't affect search.

Where the two plans meetThe security plan recommends putting the site behind Cloudflare, which can block AI crawlers by default on new sites. If the site moves there, that setting has to be checked — otherwise one plan quietly undoes the other, and AI tools may stop being able to read the site.

Structured data is a hidden label on each page that tells machines, in their own format, exactly what NeuroAIQ is, who founded it and what it offers. Today it covers the basics — the organisation and the website. The rebuild would add the founder and each of the three solutions, DRIVE™ among them.

A hand-written llms.txt replaces the auto-generated one — a plain-English summary of what NeuroAIQ does, for AI tools to read. It's an emerging convention rather than a confirmed standard, so we'd treat it as cheap insurance, not a strategy.

// structured data — simplified example { "@type": "ProfessionalService", "name": "NeuroAIQ", "slogan": "The Neuroscience of AI Adoption", "areaServed": "Australia", "founder": { "@type": "Person", "name": "[Founder]", "sameAs": "linkedin.com/in/…" }, "makesOffer": { "@type": "Service", "name": "DRIVE™ Adaptability Diagnostic" } }
Crawler names as published by each company. robots.txt is advisory — reputable crawlers follow it.

Buyer guides

Get into the guides buyers read

Contact the publishers of the 2026 Australian AI consulting guides the team reviewed and ask to be considered. Several are published by firms that include themselves — so a genuinely useful NeuroAIQ guide to choosing an AI adoption partner, clearly labelled as ours, is a fair and legitimate option too.
AI Directory

Keep the government listing current

NeuroAIQ already appears in the Australian Government AI Directory. Its description should match the new site word for word — consistency helps AI tools trust what they read.
LinkedIn

The founder's profile is the biggest asset

Founder-written posts and articles are indexed, shared, and can be cited by AI tools. A regular rhythm from the founder's own profile will do more for authority than the company page alone.
Partners and events

Turn relationships into links

A link from Sunway University's site, event listings on the platforms people browse, and speaking or podcast appearances — each one is a mention from somewhere that isn't NeuroAIQ, which is exactly what builds authority.
The strongest move availableIf any DRIVE™ findings can be shared — even de-identified and in aggregate — original data is what journalists, guide writers and AI tools most like to cite. A short "what we're seeing in AI adoption" piece could earn more links than months of articles.

Part seven · only if NeuroAIQ is comfortable with it

Paid advertising — where it'd genuinely help

Search takes months to build. Advertising works from day one but stops the day spending does. Used carefully, it fills the gap while the rest of this plan matures — and it answers a useful question fast: which message brings in the right enquiries.

Start here

Google Search ads

Best for: catching people already searching for an AI adoption assessment or help.

The catch: niche searches mean small volumes — but the people searching are close to ready.

Then

LinkedIn ads

Best for: reaching exact roles — CEOs, founders, heads of transformation, HR leaders — by company size and industry.

The catch: typically the most expensive per click, and it needs a strong offer. The Self-Check is that offer.

Once there's traffic

Meta — Facebook and Instagram

Best for: owner-operators of smaller businesses, promoting events, and reminding past visitors to come back.

The catch: weaker at targeting by job title, so it works best on people who've already visited.

Why Meta earns its place. NeuroAIQ's main market is small and medium organisations, where the buyer is often the owner, spending their own money. Meta reaches those owners at a fraction of LinkedIn's cost — and it's one of the cheapest ways to stay in front of people who visited the site but didn't enquire.

LinkedIn's Thought Leader Ads let NeuroAIQ promote the founder's own posts to a chosen audience. They appear as the founder's own post, marked as promoted — a person sharing expertise, which suits a firm selling trust.

ChannelSuggested testJudged on
Google Search$1,000–$1,500 a monthCost per qualified enquiry, over six weeks
LinkedIn$2,000–$3,000 a monthSelf-Check completions and booked conversations
Meta$500–$1,000 a monthReturn visits and event sign-ups
Ground rulesOne channel at a time, a fixed budget agreed first, and a stop point decided before starting. Advertising pixels go in through Tag Manager so they can be switched off in one step, no enquiry details are ever passed to an ad platform, and the privacy policy is updated before anything goes live.
Budgets are indicative, in AUD excluding GST, and would be confirmed with NeuroAIQ before any spend.
